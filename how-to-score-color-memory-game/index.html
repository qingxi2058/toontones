Score guide

What your ToonTones score means.

A good color memory score is not about naming the color. It is about matching the target shade closely enough across hue, saturation, and lightness.

Play and get a score Open the accuracy test

The three parts of a color guess

Hue

Hue is the color family. A tiny hue miss can turn yellow into orange or blue into violet.

Saturation

Saturation controls whether the color feels vivid, flat, soft, or gray.

Lightness

Lightness controls brightness. This is where many strong guesses lose points.

How to improve your score

Set hue first

Get the color family close before adjusting brightness or vividness.

Then lower saturation

Most remembered colors are less neon than your first guess.

Finish with lightness

Small brightness changes often decide whether a guess feels close or wrong.

What a good color memory score means

A high score means your final shade is close to the target across the whole color space, not just close in name. Two guesses can both be "blue" while one is much closer because it matches the same depth and vividness.

Scores above 8 usually mean the hue is right and the remaining miss is saturation or lightness. Scores above 9 mean the guess is visually close enough that many players would struggle to separate it from the target without direct comparison.

Low scores are useful too. They show whether your memory drifted to a different color family, whether the screen made the target look brighter, or whether you rushed before forming a stable mental reference.
